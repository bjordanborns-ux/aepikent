Who we are

A fraternity worth the four years, and the forty after.

Since 1949, Phi Deuteron has brought together Kent State men from aviation, engineering, nursing, business, and every corner of campus. We hold one another accountable, serve the community around us, and make a large university feel like home.

Alpha Epsilon Pi is the Jewish fraternity, but our doors are open to every man of good character who shares our values, regardless of religion.

Our history

More than seventy-five years of Phi Deuteron.

From our first colonization in 1949 to today’s growing colony, generations of brothers have carried Alpha Epsilon Pi forward at Kent State.

April 9, 1949

Phi Deuteron begins

Alpha Epsilon Pi’s Phi Deuteron Chapter was first colonized as the third fraternity established on Kent State’s campus. Its first house stood at 230 East Main Street.

1979

A preserved charter and a new beginning

After the chapter became inactive amid nationwide challenges for Greek life during the Vietnam era, alumni helped restart Phi Deuteron. The chapter was reactivated on January 20 and re-chartered on April 19, with Fred Binstock serving as its first Master in almost a decade. Its new home was 216 University Drive.

1991–2000

Homes across Kent

Phi Deuteron lived at several addresses during the decade, including 323 E. School St., 802 S. Depeyster St., 424 E. Main St., 306 E. Summit St., and residences on North and South Lincoln Streets.

1994–2008

The legacy of “Doc Whiz”

Dr. Robert “Doc Whiz” Zuckerman became Alpha Epsilon Pi’s oldest pledge in 1994. He remained closely involved with his pledge brothers and served as Faculty and Alumni Advisor until his unexpected death in 2008. His loss was deeply felt, and his name remains held in the highest regard locally and nationally.

2008

Latkefest begins

Alumnus Victor Newman founded Latkefest, which grew into a respected campus philanthropy attended by hundreds. Following Doc’s passing, Mike Levinstein and Sol Factor served as Alumni and Faculty Advisors; Sol became an honorary Phi Deuteron brother in 2010.

2019–2022

Seventy years and another rebuilding

Michael Pollak and Terry Hugo organized Phi Deuteron’s 70th anniversary in 2019, welcoming more than 80 alumni from nearly every decade back to campus. After losing its house during the COVID-19 pandemic, the chapter returned to Main Street. In 2022, Andrew Aronoff and Mat Kirlis, alongside advisors Michael Pollak and Joel Black, helped revive old traditions while building new ones.

December 7, 2024

Phi Deuteron returns

After the chapter closed in spring 2024, Regional Director and alumnus Matt Cohen, advisor Joel Black, and brother Kevin Bluffestone worked to assemble a new founding group. Kevin became Master and a Founding Father alongside Lieutenant Master Arye Carlson, Scribe Blake Jordan-Borns, and Rush Chair Dov Kovach. Phi Deuteron officially returned to campus as a colony.

July 1, 2026

A new home on Summit Street

The colony moved into its current home at 319 E. Summit St. The house gives the growing brotherhood a place to preserve traditions, create new ones, and continue working toward becoming a fully re-established chapter.

In memoriam

Brother Jim Fleischer ’93

Jim Fleischer attended Kent State, became a brother of Phi Deuteron, and graduated in 1993. He devoted his adult life to Alpha Epsilon Pi as a Chapter Consultant, Chapter Advisor, Regional Governor, Supreme Governor, Assistant Executive Director, Chief Operating Officer, and—beginning in 2018—the fraternity’s Chief Executive Officer.

In 2019, AEPi’s Supreme Council awarded Jim the Order of the Lion, the fraternity’s highest honor, in recognition of his exemplary dedication and service. He was also a husband, father of two AEPi brothers at Indiana University, and a brother remembered personally for his warmth, his devotion to the fraternity, and his favorite Long Island Iced Tea from Ray’s Place.

Brother Fleischer passed away on October 9, 2021, after a three-year battle with cancer. His memory will always be a blessing.

Frequently asked questions

Fraternity life at Kent State

Quick answers for students and families exploring Alpha Epsilon Pi and Greek life in Kent, Ohio.

Who can join Alpha Epsilon Pi at Kent State?

Rush is open to any Kent State man of good character who shares our values. AEPi is proudly the Jewish fraternity, and membership is open regardless of religion.

Where is the AEPi chapter house in Kent, Ohio?

Our Phi Deuteron chapter house is at 319 E Summit St, Kent, OH 44240, near the Kent State University campus.

How do I rush AEPi at Kent State?

Come to a casual recruitment event, meet the brothers, and talk with chapter leadership. Follow @aepikent on Instagram for the current rush schedule.

What does the Phi Deuteron Chapter value?

Our chapter centers brotherhood, leadership, service, academics, personal responsibility, and Jewish values while serving Kent State and the wider Kent community.
